# Keeper — company and website context

Background for the video generator and anyone briefing it. Everything here matches the live website as of 2 Oct 2026. If the site says something different later, the site is right.

## In one sentence

Keeper builds app-connected luggage lockers in the places travellers already are, billed by the minute with a daily price cap, starting in Berlin.

## What the company actually does

Keeper is a Berlin startup building a connected network of smart lockers for short-term storage in cities. Luggage is the starting point.

- **The product:** a steel locker unit with 21 compartments in three sizes, connected to the internet. Travellers find it in the Keeper app, reserve a compartment, open it from their phone, and pay only for the minutes they used.
- **Where the lockers go:** private property where people arrive with bags — hotels, shopping centres, venues and festivals, trade fairs, tourist destinations and selected transit properties. Not on the pavement.
- **Who pays:** the traveller, per minute. The location hosting the locker gives floor space and earns 20% of the storage revenue from its site. Keeper owns the hardware, the app, the payments and the customer relationship.
- **Longer-term idea:** the same installed lockers can later serve other short-stay storage needs (shopping, hospitality, venues) without building a second network.

Keeper is **not** a parcel-locker network, not a bag-drop-at-a-shop marketplace, and not an event-only locker rental.

## The problem it solves

People in cities carry things they don't want to carry.

- Hotel check-out at 11, train at 9pm — ten hours with a suitcase.
- Straight off a flight, wanting to see the city instead of a left-luggage counter.
- Arriving at a festival from the station with a bag and nowhere to put it.
- Airbnb check-in at 4pm, the last one ended at 11am.

Today's options are thin: hand the bag to a shop employee and hope, or find a coin locker at the station and pay for a block of hours you may not need. Neither shows what's free before you walk there, and neither charges for just the time used.

## How it works (three steps)

1. **Find** — see Keeper locations and which compartments are free, before setting off.
2. **Lock it up** — reserve a compartment and open it from your phone. No keys, no coins, no counter.
3. **Pay the minute** — billed by the minute from the moment the door closes, and never more than the day rate.

## Pricing

| Size | Fits | Per minute | Day rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | Backpack, tote, camera bag | 3c | €4.50 |
| Medium | Cabin case, sports bag | 3.5c | €5.25 |
| Large | Checked suitcase, two bags | 4c | €6.00 |

- Per-minute billing runs for the first 150 minutes (two and a half hours). After that the day rate applies and the price stops rising for the day.
- No unlock fee, no minimum, no subscription needed.
- Planned, not available yet: **Keeper Pass** (€5 a month, then €2 per storage day) and **Keeper Credit** (prepaid balance at 20% off, from €10).

## Where things stand

Keeper is **pre-launch**. The website states plainly that no locker is installed and nothing is bookable yet; the first Berlin units are being built. No launch date and no locations are announced. The call to action is **joining the waitlist / early access**.

So the video should promise what is coming ("Starting in Berlin", "Join early access"), not show a service people can use today.

## Reading the website: what to watch for

A scraper will pick up a few things that should **not** flow into the video as they are.

- **The logo on the site is out of date.** The site still shows Keevy next to the name. The current logo is the arch-and-path symbol in `brand/logo/`. Use the kit, not the site, for the logo and the splash.
- **Locker photos on the site are AI-generated illustrations**, labelled as such. No Keeper locker is installed anywhere yet. Don't present them as real installations.
- **The traveller quotes are illustrative scenarios, not customer reviews.** The site says so. There are no customers yet — never show them as testimonials.
- **Future tense matters.** The site says "you'll unlock", "will work". Keep that; don't turn it into "available now".
- **Not claimed anywhere, so don't add:** insurance cover, 24/7 support, guaranteed response times, named locations, a launch date, other cities.
- **Prices:** only the ones in the table above. Older material with a €1 unlock fee, "€3 a day" or "€5.90" is outdated.

## Questions the site answers (FAQ, condensed)

- **What is Keeper?** A connected network for short-term storage in the city, starting with luggage. Find a locker, unlock it from your phone, pay for the time used.
- **What happens after two and a half hours?** The day rate applies and nothing more is charged that day.
- **How do I open a locker?** Through the Keeper app — no keys, coins or counter. Not live yet.
- **Where are the lockers?** Starting in Berlin on private property. Addresses are published only once confirmed and operating.
- **Is my luggage safe?** Steel compartments, individually locked, openable only from the session that reserved them; access events are recorded.
- **Can my hotel or venue host one?** Yes — 20% of on-site storage revenue plus a free Partner Dashboard.
- **When does it launch?** Starting in Berlin; no date announced. Early access members hear first.
